# One structuring entry, and the role on the location

[ADR-0005](0005-one-operation-template-and-one-publication-plan.md) gave pack and extract one operation template and one stop signal. It left failure structuring spread over about thirty catch sites, each of which decided for itself whether an `IOException` was a stop, an already structured failure, or a raw source or destination fault. Those decisions had drifted. A worker's stop (`OrderedWorkerRunner.StoppedWork`) was read as SOURCE `operation.source-io` by pack workers and as DESTINATION `operation.destination-io` by extraction workers; the outcome came out right only because the runner's skip rule discards later ordinals. The same misreading lost a Residual Artifact: `TransformStage` folded a stopped worker's spool cleanup failure into the bogus failure, and the runner then discarded it. Four worker throwable mappers disagreed with each other and with the template. `IoContext` derived the location slot from the Failure Kind, so pack, which anchored almost every context at its destination, reported SOURCE failures with the destination as their `archive`, and the same `io.resource-capacity` moved between `archive` and `artifact` depending on the phase.

## Decision

1. **Two stop signals, one supertype.** A sealed, package-private `StopSignal extends IOException` permits `OperationStopped` and `WorkerStopped` (the former `StoppedWork`, now at package level in `internal.io`). Both are created fresh on every throw. A stop is never a Failure Kind.
2. **One structuring entry.** `IoContext.structure(Throwable)` is where every catch site hands its throwable over, including a site with a specific identifier (`structure(failure, identifier)`, for example `source.attributes` or `extraction.destination-io`). It rethrows either stop and any `Error` other than `AssertionError`, a `VirtualMachineError` among them, matching the template. It keeps an `ArchiveException`, maps any other `IOException` by the location's role, and makes a `RuntimeException`, an `AssertionError` or any other throwable INTERNAL `operation.internal-failure`. No site judges whether a stop can reach it, and the hand-written `catch (OperationStopped) { throw … }` clauses are gone. The four worker mappers (`OrderedWorkerRunner.structured` and `cleanupFailure`, `PublicationTransaction.acceptWorkerFailure`, `TransformStage.failure`) and the facade's near-copy, `BethesdaArchives.failure`, are deleted. `IoContext`'s raw `failure(kind, identifier, cause)` constructor keeps the tripwire: it refuses a stop as a cause, as a backstop for code that bypasses the entry.
3. **A stopped worker's cleanup evidence is settled by the runner.** A worker catch site closes its resources first and then calls `IoContext.settle(failure, cleanup)`. A stop carries each cleanup `ArchiveException` with `addSuppressed`; any other failure folds with them. The runner's `execute` copies a stop's suppressed failures onto the completion, and the runner accepts them into the session in plan order, whether the stopped outcome is drained by `close()` or consumed as skipped and closed by the coordinator. The session's normal selection decides Primary or Secondary. Workers still never call the session.
4. **The role is on the location.** Every `IoContext` is an Operation Source or an Operation Destination. The role picks the `DiagnosticLocation` slot for every Failure Kind: a source fills `archive`, a destination fills `artifact`. SOURCE and DESTINATION are derived from the role only (`roleFailure` and the entry); the raw constructor rejects either kind passed explicitly, so a SOURCE failure located at a destination cannot be written.
5. **A path's side is fixed by its place in the operation, not by the direction of the I/O.** Reading the operation's own staged output is a destination read: pack readback opens its staged part at a destination through `ArchiveReaders.open(IoContext, …)`, and Content Sharing compares through `StagedFile.read`, whose context is publication's. A fault there is DESTINATION `operation.destination-io`, and a readback FORMAT failure names the part in `artifact`. Scratch spools are destinations even when the caller's context is a source.
6. **Pack's source-side failures are located at the source they read.** Each `PackSources.Entry` carries its source location: a loose file or a repacked archive at its path, or a generated source, which has no path and is located by its entry ordinal and name with both path slots empty (so `IoContext`'s path is optional). Per-entry PROCESSING contexts come from that location, and a payload receives its entry's PROCESSING location. `PackSources`' shape and attribute checks are located at the path they examined. `OrderedWorkerRunner` takes a per-ordinal `IoContext` locator instead of an ordinal function. Destination-side contexts (the budget, publication, targets, scratch) are destinations, and publication relocates a budget failure through `IoContext.relocated` instead of rebuilding its location by hand.
7. **The template's fallback is INTERNAL.** A raw `IOException` that escapes the body of `OperationSession.run` becomes INTERNAL `operation.internal-failure` at the interrupted phase: every catch site structures its own faults, so an unstructured one at the template is a JBSA bug, not a fault whose side the template could know. Known leaks are structured where they occur; pack planning's `Files.walkFileTree` fault is SOURCE at the walked root.
8. **Registered resources declare their location.** `register(resource, location)` replaces `register(resource)`. A raw close failure is structured through the entry at that location, at CLEANUP with no ordinal. The open source archive is a source at the archive; the pack budget and the extraction budget are destinations at their path, and the stabilization spool is a destination at its scratch parent, because its spill file does not exist yet when it registers. A resource that structures its own close failures, such as `SpillBuffer`, keeps its evidence.

Some sites still build a role failure directly instead of handing a throwable to the entry, and the raw tripwire still rejects a stop at each of them:

- Primitives that structure their own evidence (decision 8): `SpillBuffer`'s cleanup and abort, and `TargetPaths.close`'s pin release. Their contract throws only `ArchiveException`, and what they catch is a provider fault from a channel close, a file delete or a pin release. `SpillBuffer.read` also reclassifies any failure of reading its own scratch, a FORMAT end-of-file among them, as DESTINATION `operation.destination-io`: scratch is owned output even while it is replayed, so a short read there is not archive damage.
- The public query facade (`BethesdaArchives`), which runs under no session, so no stop can reach it, and which cannot name the package-private `StopSignal` the entry declares.
- A `SecurityException` denying access to a path. The entry reads any `RuntimeException` as INTERNAL, so a site that catches one makes it the location's I/O failure with `ioFailure` or `roleFailure` instead.

## Consequences

No conformance golden or Semantic Expectation changes: the BSA, BA2 and DDS public observers deliberately omit `archive` and `artifact`. The location pins that encoded the old kind-derived rule were rewritten to the role rule: `ExactIoTest` now checks both roles in both directions, and `RawLz4ExhaustionTest`'s preflight budget lines report `artifact=large.ba2` and `artifact=scratch.ba2`, as its processing lines already did. `ParallelPackTest` now compares failure locations across runs with different destinations.

Failure Kinds change only where no public-seam test provoked them before: a readback or Content Sharing read fault becomes DESTINATION; a non-fatal `Error` other than an assertion in a pack or extraction worker propagates, as it does from the template, instead of becoming INTERNAL; a non-structured I/O fault drained from an unconsumed extraction worker is DESTINATION at its part rather than SOURCE; a raw `IOException` reaching the template becomes INTERNAL instead of SOURCE; and a structured failure raised inside a source attribute read keeps its own identifier instead of being rewrapped as `source.attributes`. A stopped pack worker's residual spool path now reaches the session, where it used to be dropped.

Relocation changes deterministic order among failures that tie on phase and Logical Plan Order, and in principle Primary Failure key 4 of JBSA-OPS-006, the structured location. `FailureRetention` compares a location's `archive`, then its entry ordinal and name, then its field and span, then its `artifact`, with present members first. Among failures with the same phase, ordinal and identifier, one located at a source therefore precedes one located at a generated source, which precedes one located at a destination. Before this decision most pack failures were located in `archive` at the destination, so such ties fell to the other keys. `IoContextTest` pins the new order.

This extends ADR-0005 rather than superseding it: the template, the publication plan and the stop signal's meaning are unchanged, and ADR-0005 §1 now describes the stop-signal supertype and the rethrowing entry instead of per-site rethrow clauses. The specification's Failure Kind table and JBSA-OPS-005 record the Operation Source and Operation Destination rule (specification `0.22.0`).
